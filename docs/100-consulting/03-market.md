# Market scan — plant care and plant diagnosis apps

**Written by** 100 Consulting, run 1 (`photo-assessment`). **Date checked: 2026-08-24.**
**Corrected:** 2026-08-24, second pass. No new research was done; two claims were repaired.
**Read next by** 200 Product.

This is the only role on the line that looks outside the project. Every fact below carries a link
and the date it was checked. Anything that could not be checked against a first-party source is
marked **unverified**, and stays marked.

**First-party** means the seller's own page or the app store listing. **Third-party** means somebody
writing about the product. Prices from blogs were not used. Where an app store shows a price range
rather than one price, the range is copied as it appears, because stores list several products under
one name.

### Where nothing was found

**Hungary — no Hungarian-made plant care or plant diagnosis app was found.** Searches were run in
Hungarian for a houseplant watering-reminder app and for a Hungarian-developed plant disease
diagnosis app. What came back were foreign apps translated into Hungarian — Blossom (Italy), Plantix
(Germany), Pl@ntNet (France) — and Hungarian magazine articles recommending those same foreign apps.

**This is "not found", not "does not exist".** Two searches in one language do not prove absence. It
is recorded as an observation, and it should be re-checked before anyone builds a plan on it.

### What the table shows, in three lines

1. **Everyone identifies plants. Almost nobody schedules the fix.** Identification is the crowded
   part of the market. The step from "here is what is wrong" to "here is a task for Thursday" is
   where the products get thin.
2. **The Hungarian market is served by translation, not by design.** The two largest care apps,
   PictureThis and Planta, do not ship Hungarian at all.
3. **Everything is a subscription, and in Hungary it is expensive.** Blossom's yearly price of
   22 990–29 990 Ft is real money for a person with a few pot plants in an apartment. No exchange
   rate was checked, so no comparison to the dollar prices is made here.

## 2. Worth considering later

**Nothing in this section enters run 1, and nothing here outranks a backbone feature.** It is a list
for the owner to choose from later, written down now so that the thinking is not lost. Each line is
an idea, who it is for, and why it might beat what already exists.

| # | The idea | Who it is for | Why it might beat what exists |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| W-1 | **Hungarian as a designed language, not a translation.** Plant names, symptom words and care advice written for Hungarian, including the everyday names people actually use, such as `legénypálma` and `pénznövény`. **Part of this is no longer "later":** `factory/feature.md` decides Hungarian and English ship in run 1. What stays on this list is the *depth* — writing the plant and symptom words for Hungarian instead of translating them | People in Hungary who keep houseplants and do not read English comfortably | PictureThis and Planta, the two biggest care apps, do not ship Hungarian at all. Blossom does, at 22 990–29 990 Ft a year |
| W-2 | **Pay for what you use, instead of a subscription.** A small pack of assessments, bought once, no renewal | Someone with a few plants who needs an assessment three or four times a year | Every product in the table above except the two free ones charges a repeating fee. A person who uses it a handful of times a year pays a full year's subscription for those few uses |
| W-3 | **Show the reason, not only the answer.** The assessment says what in the photo it looked at — "the brown is on the leaf edge, not in the middle" — so the user can disagree with it | Anyone who does not trust an app that just states a conclusion | None of the products checked shows its reasoning to the user. It also makes the `unsure` and `cannot-tell` bands from D-02 easier to believe |
| W-4 | **A second opinion, and say when the two disagree.** Run the same photo past a specialist API such as `plant.health` as well as the general model, and tell the user plainly when they do not agree | Anyone about to do something that cannot be undone, such as repotting or cutting roots | Turns uncertainty into a feature instead of hiding it. `plant.health` is €0.05 a credit and covers 548 classes, so the second opinion is cheap. Note it also adds a second outside dependency and a second bill |
| W-5 | **Delete and export everything in one tap.** The user can see every photo the app holds, remove them all, and take a copy | Anyone storing photos of the inside of their home | GDPR duties turned into a visible feature rather than a page of text. None of the products checked advertises this. Run 1 already carries the smaller half of it: 180-day deletion and delete-on-demand |
| W-6 | **Take the photo now, assess when there is a network.** The photo is captured and queued, and the assessment runs when the phone is back online | Anyone in a flat with weak signal, or in a greenhouse or a cellar | The moment of noticing a sick plant and the moment of having a good connection are not always the same moment |
| W-7 | **A light and placement check from the same photo.** The photo already shows the room. Use it to say whether the plant is standing in the right place | The same user, at the same moment | Backbone feature 3 is placement advice. Greg does this with a light meter as a separate step. Doing it from the photo already taken removes a step. **Note:** this is close to an approved backbone feature, so it is a *way of building* item 3, not a new feature |

**How an idea leaves this list.** The owner picks it, writes it into a copy of `factory/feature.md`
as the one feature of a later run, and the line runs again. That is the only route.
